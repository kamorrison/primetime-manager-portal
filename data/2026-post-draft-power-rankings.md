## 1. JD 316 — The cleanest contender in the room—until somebody asks the bench to do something

**Manager:** Jehu  
**Rating:** 72.01 · **Tier:** Tier 1 — Championship Favorites · **Draft grade:** B  
**Strongest room:** WR · **Weakest room:** DEPTH

JD 316 takes the first post-draft throne because the premium structure is exactly what a Superflex roster is supposed to look like: Drake Maye and Bo Nix at the controls, CeeDee Lamb and Nico Collins creating the weekly ceiling, and enough early skill-position investment to avoid living on prayer candles.

The model gives Jehu an 86.50 WR score and an 81.71 QB score, the kind of two-column foundation that keeps a lineup dangerous even when one position has a quiet Sunday. Travis Etienne and D'Andre Swift are not carrying the brand here; they are being asked to keep the floor intact while the passing game commits property damage. Tua Tagovailoa at pick 136 is also a legitimate third-quarterback contingency rather than a decorative QB3.

The hard fact is a 47.61 depth score. Aaron Jones, Tyler Allgeier, rookies, and late tight ends constitute insurance, but this is not a bench that should be allowed near open flame. JD 316 is ranked first because the starting case is powerful—not because the roster is bulletproof. One or two premium misses and the championship favorite turns into a very expensive group chat explanation.

- No. 1 overall model rating: 72.01
- WR score: 86.50
